## Persistence, provenance, and identity

Current behavior: `fudaba_cards` is the canonical card table. The unification migration adds numeric `card_number` for compatibility URLs and immutable provenance vocabulary `exchange`, `guest`, and `legacy` (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/20260819000000_namecard_unification_foundation.sql:18`). Registered cards use string IDs, generally UUIDs; numeric compatibility IDs are a separate identity surface. Old `cards` and `card_emojis` tables are preserved and guarded against writes by the legacy read-only migration (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/20260826130000_namecard_legacy_tables_read_only.sql:62`). Claims still query the historical `cards` table, so removing it would break current behavior.

The repository selects owner account, producer name, display name, primary series, structured idol selections, front/back object keys, accent, bio, trade note, availability, source URL/label/credit, media rights, publication status, revision, timestamps, and deletion timestamp (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:79`). Guest hash/IP/token attributes and anonymous reaction counters live beside the canonical row. The later guest-profile migration allows an unowned card to retain descriptive content, while requiring `trade_note IS NULL` and `available=FALSE` (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/20260820000000_namecard_guest_profile.sql:18`). Do not apply the earlier unification constraint as the final schema contract.

Registered creation derives `ownerAccountId` from `platformUser.id`; metadata writes accept no replacement owner (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/handlers/create-card.ts:72`, `apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/request.ts:26`). Producer name is card content. The editor prefills it from profile display name, then permits editing (`apps/web/app/pages/community/exchange/me/exchange-me-model.ts:57`). Source credit is descriptive attribution; it does not establish a creator account relation. Upload objects carry an account metadata value, but the selected database card fields contain no separate persistent uploader-account or creator-role relation. Guest submissions have an IP/token, not a platform owner.

Planning gaps for NC-01/05/06/07/08: no producer reading, symbolic group field, structured creative contributors, voluntary AI field, or independent uploader audit relation exists in the inspected card schema, request parser, owner/public wire shapes, and editor model. The future RFC should preserve the distinction between card署名, management ownership, upload actor, and contributor attribution.

## Validation and Wiki eligibility

Current registered cards require producer name (80 characters), display name (120), one primary series, and 1 to 20 distinct positive idol IDs. Cross-series idol selection is permitted. The SQL validator checks existence and `idol.deleted_at IS NULL`, joining agencies for names and codes; it does not enforce idol Wiki visibility, `entry_kind`, or the product's allowed role categories (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:669`). Primary series creation/update separately requires an enabled Wiki agency (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:2382`). Historical claims permit zero idols and derive an enabled series from explicit selection, target card, selected idols, then historical card (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:2578`, `:2620`).

The public Wiki catalog includes enabled entries under enabled agencies and exposes `entryKind`/`entrySubtype`; it does not restrict search entries to idols (`apps/api/src/domains/content/wiki/catalog/handlers/list-public-catalog.ts:61`). The current namecard selector consumes this whole list, filtering only agency and text (`apps/web/app/components/community/idol-multi-select.tsx:45`). Therefore a Wiki unit/story/other entry can be offered as a担当 and passes repository validation if its ID exists.

Wiki kinds are `idol`, `unit`, `story`, `other`; story subtypes are `main`, `event`, `special`, `other` (`packages/contracts/src/wiki.ts:36`). They classify content pages. They cannot prove培育偶像/对手/事务员/社长 or exclude联动/剧中剧/愚人节. `wiki_groups` and `wiki_group_members` support ordered display grouping; the selected-agency catalog expands membership into entry objects (`apps/api/src/domains/content/wiki/catalog/handlers/list-public-catalog.ts:121`). Membership is not a verified musical-unit role taxonomy. The SideM migration expressly distinguishes unit entries from idols (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/0017_wiki_entry_types.sql:13`).

Planning gaps for NC-02/03/04/26: introduce an explicit eligible角色 catalog/policy and admin correction path. Define how real combinations map to member idols, rather than treating every Wiki display group or unit content entry as a combination. The existing selector has no unmatched "other" or combination-expansion action. Collection filtering currently supports primary series, availability, and office slug; no selected-idol/group or cross-border/multi担当 policy exists in that query.

## Claims, review states, and visibility

Current publication states in SQL are `draft`, `pending`, `approving`, `published`, `hidden`, `rejected`, `withdrawn`; media rights are `unknown`, `approved`, `denied` (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/20260819000000_namecard_unification_foundation.sql:71`, `packages/contracts/src/fudaba/index.ts:299`). Claim state is independently `pending`, `approving`, `approved`, `rejected`, `cancelled` (`packages/contracts/src/fudaba/card-claims.ts:16`). Public compatibility metadata derives `unclaimed`, `pending`, `claimed` from legacy binding and claim rows. A deleted but still bound card remains claimed (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:2957`).

There is no product severe-violation `locked` state in these models. `approving` is a temporary concurrency freeze, not a sanction. Normal/hidden display is currently mixed into publication status. The owner update schema/parser only accepts content and `available`; it exposes no ordinary hide/unhide action. `available` filters willingness/availability and must not be reinterpreted as privacy.

Registered edits use expected revision, advance the revision, clear media rights to `unknown`, and move publication to `pending` (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:2418`, `:2482`). They overwrite the current row. Registered review uses `pending -> approving -> published/rejected`, an object-publication step, rollback, and reconciliation after uncertain completion. The final repository transition and audit are transactional (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:3155`). Review DTOs expose the current card and owner, not a prior approved version or image diff (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/moderation/response.ts:31`). Review notes exist for claims; registered review embeds its note in audit target text (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/moderation/handlers/admin-card-reviews.ts:121`). Compatibility rejection accepts expected revision and has no structured product reason catalog.

Legacy claims require an approved historical legacy submission, active claimant, optional owned unbound target, and no existing binding. One open/approved claim per legacy card is enforced by SQL (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/20260816193000_namecard_ownership_foundation.sql:115`). Same-ID matching produces an envelope requiring user action and administrator approval. Approval without an existing target upgrades the canonical ownerless row in place, retains `origin`, copies/publishes media, and attaches owner metadata. Approval with an existing target binds it and soft-deletes the duplicate ownerless compatibility row (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:3287`). This is a historical proof/review workflow, not exchange consent.

Public filtering differs by surface. Fudaba cards require published status, approved rights, live row, owner active/restricted with no deletion timestamp, and enabled primary agency (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:141`, `:1174`). Office detail reads apply active-office and owner eligibility, but do not require `office.is_open` when returning existing wall placements (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:1132`). Placement creation has stricter open-office rules; closure is not a universal read-privacy switch. Owner reads include unpublished/rejected cards and require matching owner and live row (`:2268`). Registered admin queue selects pending/approving rows (`:3017`); the compatibility admin list excludes rejected/withdrawn rows (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/core-repository.ts:302`).

The numeric compatibility wall counts/lists published guest/legacy rows without owner, media-rights, or enabled-series predicates (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/core-repository.ts:262`). Unowned cards therefore remain publicly visible and counted indefinitely if published. Its media handler considers compatibility-approved status sufficient for public delivery (`apps/api/src/domains/delivery/media/handlers/serve-namecard.ts:18`). NC-09/15/29 require consistent visibility across these projections and delivery paths.

## Upload and media lifecycle

Current registered creation requires both front and back, accepts at most two files, and limits each input to 8 MiB (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/handlers/create-card.ts:19`). It checks extension/MIME/decoded format, converts to WebP quality 88, checks converted byte size, and stores protected objects. Supported validation extensions include PNG/JPEG/JFIF/GIF/WebP/BMP/AVIF/HEIC/HEIF (`apps/api/src/utils/media/image-upload.ts:13`); actual decode support depends on the image adapter. Decoded dimensions are returned by conversion but are not persisted as orientation/page metadata by this card create handler.

Guest submission also requires exactly two images, limits each to 3 MiB, normalizes to WebP quality 85, generates protected JPEG thumbnails at bounds 600 by 400, and checks ordered normalized MD5 hashes for duplicates (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/guest-submissions/handlers/upload-guest-submission.ts:74`, `apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/card-media-assets.ts:13`). Normalization permits 70 million input pixels for JPEG/JFIF and 40 million otherwise; inputs above 40 million pixels request an output cap of 16 million (`apps/api/src/utils/media/normalize-uploaded-image.ts:10`). Producer name is optional for guests, although series/idols are required by submission parsing and UI. Signing in does not make this anonymous submission route bind the resulting card to the account. The public-wall upload dialog calls this guest endpoint (`apps/web/app/components/community/namecard-upload-dialog.tsx:141`).

Owner replacement accepts a single front/back side with revision fencing, writes protected media, returns the card to pending, and deletes the previous object after a confirmed commit. Compatibility-origin replacements use the namecard layout; registered exchange-origin replacements use versioned Fudaba object keys (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/handlers/upload-owned-media.ts:108`). Storage writes, ambiguous database outcomes, deletion, publication, and protection use compensation/reconciliation. Current versioned object keys prevent replacement races but do not provide durable historical card versions: old images are scheduled for cleanup.

Owner delete sets `deleted_at`, rights unknown and pending status, then deletes both images with compensation (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/handlers/delete-card.ts:35`). This directly conflicts with NC-16. Rejected/withdrawn guest rows are physically purged after three days when the guest upload path invokes cleanup (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/guest-submissions/ttl-purge.ts:6`, `apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/core-repository.ts:430`). This is neither a lock retention policy nor an unclaimed-card expiry.

Mounted guest routes support upload, token status/media reads, and pending withdrawal. Repository methods for rejected/withdrawn image replacement and resubmission exist, but `guest-submissions/routes.ts` exposes neither action; migration documentation expressly reserves them from guests (`docs/migrations/fudaba-platform.md:33`). Do not count repository helpers as completed UI/API capability.

Preview already uses `object-contain`, supports front/back switching and zoom, and thumbnail fallback loads the original (`apps/web/app/components/shared/namecard-preview.tsx:320`, `apps/web/app/pages/community/components/namecard-thumbnail.tsx:42`). NC-30 has reusable display behavior, but there is no multipage, alpha-layer, effect preset, or model asset relation in the inspected card columns/contracts/upload routes. The RFC still needs optional back, fixed-container rules, orientation/rotation, output budget, page order, and resource limits for NC-10/11/31/32/33.

## Reactions, favorites, and exchange foundation

Current anonymous emoji reactions are counters shared between compatibility and registered surfaces. Account likes/favorites are separate `(card_id, account_id)` rows with creation timestamps and idempotent insert/remove operations (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/0022_fudaba_domain.sql:214`). They do not require recipient approval. The interaction handler writes before checking the public interaction projection (`apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/cards/handlers/set-card-interaction.ts:24`); the repository insertion only checks card non-deletion and account existence (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:3876`). A 404 can therefore follow a stored interaction on an ineligible card. New collection/consent writes should check eligibility inside their transaction.

Favorites call the public card query with `favoritedByAccountId`. They contain current card data, retain public eligibility requirements, and sort by card creation time descending, not favorite/exchange time (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:1174`, `apps/api/src/domains/community/fudaba/directory/handlers/list-favorite-cards.ts:12`). The Web account workspace has a `FavoriteCollection` view. Favorites offer no durable received-version snapshot, per-entry hiding, exchange provenance, or account/card-deletion preservation. NC-25/26/27 require a new collection contract or a deliberate migration of this semantic surface.

There is a real exchange foundation: `fudaba_exchange_requests` records office, requester/recipient, wanted/offered card, note, pending/accepted/declined/cancelled status, version, timestamps. Composite FKs enforce ownership; a partial index prevents duplicate pending requests; triggers reject changes after resolution and require each update to advance version (`apps/api/migrations/postgresql/0022_fudaba_domain.sql:174`, `:323`). `createExchangeRequest` checks an office placement and ownership and inserts pending (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/fudaba-repository.ts:3821`). It does not enforce the full current public-card/account policy used by discovery.

Inspection covered the Fudaba route assembler and every capability route, the complete repository exchange section and port, Web Fudaba endpoint modules, contracts Fudaba modules, and all production references to `createExchangeRequest`. The request creator has test callers and a port declaration; no production handler calls it. No acceptance command, completed receipt/collection write, bilateral confirmation, automatic acceptance settings, temporary exchange channel, offline resend protocol, or nearby/background session is exposed in those surfaces. Existing exchange acceptance tests update SQL directly (`apps/api/tests/server/fudaba.test.ts:3505`). NC-18 through NC-24 need an application workflow and durable completion transaction. The existing office-bound FK model also cannot accept a direct share-page request without an office placement.

## Platform deletion and sharing entry points

Platform and Backoffice sessions are distinct. Owner writes use Platform auth, active account, CSRF for cookies, and rate limiting; owner reads allow restricted accounts. Packaged clients use bearer transport. Existing OAuth one-time app return codes protect login/linking; they are not transferable card-exchange credentials (`.trellis/spec/api/backend/authentication.md:17`, `:43`).

Account schema/read policy anticipates deleted accounts, but there is no account-deletion capability or repository action in the inspected identity routes and platform account repository. The account-security architecture document explicitly marks soft-delete/anonymization as unimplemented (`docs/architecture/platform-account-security.md:43`). The implemented administrator action suspends/restores accounts with session revocation; it does not detach cards (`apps/api/src/infra/db/repositories/platform-account-repository.ts:2275`). Owner/claim FKs use `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, so physical account deletion cannot provide NC-28.

An existing design decision says public card/office content remains displayed after account deletion and producer署名 remains content (`docs/architecture/platform-account-security.md:89`, `:117`). NC-28/29 now require automatic unbinding and eventual public/search/stat removal. The new RFC must reconcile that earlier prose with these requirements, the owner-dependent public query, owner-layer constraints, legacy bindings, and exchange composite ownership FKs.

Current Web route metadata has community wall, exchange directory, office slug, owner workspace, and token submission status pages (`apps/web/app/route-metadata.ts:153`). The wall uses a detail dialog rather than a public card share route. Inspected meta exports provide titles/descriptions, with no card-specific OGP output (`apps/web/app/pages/community/index.tsx:54`, `apps/web/app/pages/community/exchange/community-exchange-page.tsx:429`). The upload dialog's clipboard link is a private token receipt/status link, not public sharing (`apps/web/app/components/community/namecard-upload-dialog.tsx:174`). Source-wide searches for `og:`, `navigator.share`, `QRCode`, and share payload/card symbols found no implemented card share pipeline in the three workspaces. Main-agent research must establish the separate sharing task's proposed interfaces. Existing public delivery, typed path builders, route metadata/frontend delivery inventory, dialog, and public card DTOs are integration entry points for NC-17.

## Product decisions the RFC still needs

- Collection version semantics: preserve the approved version received, show the latest approved version, or provide an explicit update action. Current favorites show current public data; media replacements remove old images. NC-16 needs a retention model under any option.
- Editing publication: decide whether the prior approved version stays visible/exchangeable during review and whether consent fixes exact approved versions. Current edits immediately remove public eligibility. Define a meaningful comparison threshold without automatically approving subtle edits.
- Sanction lock: define who locks/unlocks, whether owner/recipient can still read, whether exchange initiation stops, appeal workflow, retention, and what surviving collection entries show after purge. `approving` cannot supply these rules.
- Card hidden versus collection-entry hidden: maintain separate scopes. The PRD already permits owner-initiated exchange of hidden cards and includes them in statistics; the RFC must specify recipient access and public share resolution without revealing them in discovery.
- Unbinding and historical reclaims: decide whether unbound cards can be claimed again, who proves ownership, how a new account relates to earlier exchange attribution, and whether old approved claim/binding rows remain historical or continue blocking claims.
- Claim period and statistics: specify expiry start, cutoff, exceptions, and metrics. Current published ownerless compatibility cards never expire; terminal guest purge is unrelated.
- Eligibility curation: define supported角色 and combination mappings separately from Wiki page kinds, including "other" moderation. Do not infer exclusions from current enabled flags.
- Voluntary AI attribution and creative credit: decide editable scope, proof for linked contributor accounts, external URL validation, and how attribution survives account deletion. AI suspicion is not a rejection reason under NC-07.
- Image/page/effect budgets: NC-11's 1 MB is an example, not an established limit. Set accepted formats, input/output/per-card caps, optional-back behavior, page count/order, alpha compatibility, and fallback representation for mechanisms.
- Duplicate holding: define uniqueness by canonical card identity or approved version and how migrated favorites count. Existing duplicate-pending exchange prevention does not prevent repeated completed holdings.
